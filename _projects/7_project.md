When a wet porous material dries, the evaporating liquid does not retreat as a smooth front. Air invades the pore network in irregular, fractal-like clusters, leaving isolated pockets of liquid behind. This pattern controls how fast the material dries and where dissolved salts or particles end up. I study this with Prof. [Pietro de Anna](https://pietrodeanna.org/pda/) at the University of Lausanne.

Our micromodels are made from NOA-81, a UV-curable adhesive that is completely vapour-tight (unlike PDMS), so evaporation happens only at the open boundary. This lets us run multi-day experiments on bacterial growth during drying and on salt crystallisation in the pores.
