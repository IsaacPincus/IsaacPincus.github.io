Parametrised curves

A curve in space is a map t ↦ φ(t) = (x(t), y(t), z(t)) from an interval onto ℝ³. Drag the slider to move along the curve. The green arrow is the position vector φ(t); the red arrow is the velocity φ′(t), and the purple arrow is the unit tangent T(t) = φ′/|φ′|.

Arrows at the point
t = 0.00

Type any expression MATLAB would accept: sin, cos, exp, sqrt, abs, pi, and ^ for powers. The velocity is found numerically, so you don't need to supply derivatives.

Double integrals

The integral ∫∫ f(x,y) dx dy is the volume under the surface z = f(x,y). Doing the x-integral first gives the area A(y) of one slice; integrating those areas over y sweeps out the volume. Drag the slider to move the slice.

y = 0.00

The edges can depend on y: try 0 and y for a triangle, or -sqrt(1-y^2) and sqrt(1-y^2) for a disc. Areas and volumes here are computed numerically (Simpson's rule in x, trapezoid in y), so you can check your hand-worked answers against them.
